# Anime 3D characters

How the app makes an anime character in 3D: one call, three doors, and what the pieces are.

## One call

`model3d.anime.make` `{workspace, name, traits?, base_revision?}` (MCP; `POST /api/v1/model3d/anime`; the Studio 3D panel). No GPU, a few seconds.

`traits`: `style` (`anime`, the default, or `real`: see **Realistic style**), `gender` (`girl|boy|man`, and `woman` for a real style; the head shape follows it: `girl` a wide soft egg with a small V chin, `boy` a longer head with a clean V to a point, `man` a long narrow head with a brow ridge, straight jaw lines to a flat chin and a thick neck, face shape `sharp` by default), `body` (`slim|average|tall`), `height` (m), `skin`, `eye_color`, `lash_color` (default: the hair colour darkened), `hair_style` (`none|short|bangs|bob|long|twin_tails|spiky|ponytail|bun`), `hair_color`, `outfit` (`school|casual|sport`), `outfit_colors` `{top, bottom, shoes, accent}`, `blush`, `face_style` (`anime`: almond eyes, the default; `cute`: the round-eyed mascot face), `face_shape` (`soft|sharp`), `face_source` (`program`, the default, zero GPU; `qwen`: a painted bald face as the kit base, see below), `face_image` (with `qwen`: a keyed bald picture of this workspace), `face` (extra `blob_face` knobs: `nose`, `brows`, `line`, `ink`), `clips`.

### The two face sources

- **`program`**: `blob_face_anime_head` draws the face from traits in ~15 s on CPU. Calibrated on painted anime heads (each eye ≈ 17 % of the face width, 9 % of the skull height, almond, outer corner higher; eye line 58 % down the skull; mouth 84 %), heavy upper lash with a tail, iris taller than the opening and shaded under the lid, limbal ring, three highlights, tapered brows per expression. Any skin, eye and lash colour; `face_shape` tilts and narrows the eyes.
- **`qwen`**: a bald anime face painted by Qwen (`generation.image`, `qwen_image_21_turbo`, prompt: *"Completely bald head, no hair at all, head and neck only, front view, looking straight at the viewer, neutral calm expression, mouth closed, large anime eyes, clean 2000s TV anime cel shading, solid pure white background"*), keyed with `image.remove_background` and passed as `face_image`. `blob_face_qwen` measures it (the anime landmark model gives 0 points on bald heads, so the silhouette prior is used: eye line 0.7 of crown→chin, mouth 0.9, refined by the flat rig's hinted search only when it finds a plausible pair), cuts the two painted eyes with a feathered edge, and pastes them inside the drawn openings of the `anime_head` face (an eyes-only hybrid): lids, lashes, crease, brows, nose, mouth, blush and blink are the drawn ones, so the eleven expressions are as consistent as the drawn style, nothing of the painting is lit twice and no contour of the painting lands on the skull. The painting's skin is sampled and given to the body. Why not the whole painting or Qwen-edited expressions: pasting the painting re-lights its shading and projects its contours onto the cheeks; separately edited expressions drift (measured with the flat rig on `bald_hana_31`: eyes move 0.8–2.5 % of the picture, the mouth 0.1–3.2 %, and the eye size changes ±35 % between neutral and surprised). Limit: the painted eye cannot enlarge for `surprised`.

Both sources give the same kit shape (eleven poses, nine visemes, a blink, `fit`, `shape: mesh`, `skin`), so a scene cannot tell them apart.

Returns `rigged_file`, `file`, `faceAnchor` `{center, radii}`, `traits` (the body), `kit_id`, `poses`, `preview`, `face` `{fit, taper, poses}`, `character` (the traits as resolved).

Then, in a scene: bind `rigged_file` to a model slot (`fit.heightM` = the height), `world3d.scene.face {object_id, kit_id, center, radii}` (the kit carries `fit` and `taper`, nothing else to pass; `pose` picks an expression, `expressions` changes it during the shot), `world3d.scene.talk` for the lines. Toon: `renderLook: "toon"`, `toon {shade: "anime", steps: 2, outline: 3}`. Close-ups: camera within about 60° of the face.

## What it is made of

- **Body**: `model3d.blob.make` traits `family: "anime", mesh: "loft"` (`blob_loft`): one smooth lofted mesh, tapered limbs, a real neck, an egg head with a jaw and ears, fingers and a thumb, shoes with a sole, a closed pleated skirt or trousers or shorts, collar, bow or tie; the `outfit` picks sleeve and leg lengths and the shoe style. Rigged by the automatic humanoid rig (silhouette landmarks, geodesic weights, the Mixamo-style bone names, so clips and Meshy retargets keep working).
- **Hair**: `blob_hair`, curved clumps from a quadratic curve per strand (root on a helmet, pull point, tip) lofted as flattened tubes, written as the GLB's second primitive (material `hair`): the toon look adds a view-space gloss band on it. No spring bones: the rig's skeleton is fixed, so hair follows the Head bone rigidly.
- **Head**: the lofted skull (`blob_loft.SKULL`, read by `skull_profile`): a table MEASURED from Qwen-drawn bald anime head sheets (front and side silhouettes; crown v = 1 to chin v = 0: half-width, front depth, back depth per height, Catmull-Rom between rows, superellipse rings). An anime head is about 0.8 as wide as it is tall (the layout's head `width` is 0.116 of the height, not a ball's 0.138), its widest ring sits at 60 % of the height (0.4 down from the crown), the crown is a flat dome and the jaw is a V down to a small chin; the earlier sphere-over-a-cone was a balloon. A small nose ridge at the drawn nose's height and ears at the eye line (none under `long`, `bob` and `twin_tails`, which fall over them), with a `face` primitive (the skull's front from the chin to the hairline, a hair's breadth outside it) whose UVs project the kit picture from the front (`fit`). The picture is painted into that primitive's material (`faceDecal.shape: "mesh"`, `skin` filled under the drawing), so the face is lit and cel-shaded with the body and outlined with the head, like an old game's face texture; expressions swap the picture. Correct at 0–60°, compressed at 90°, nothing at 180° (the hair carries the back).
- **Face**: `characters.face.make` style `anime_head` (`blob_face_anime_head`) or the painted `qwen` source (`blob_face_qwen`), both with eleven expressions (`neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, smug, shy, sleepy, suspicious, tired, shout`), nine visemes and a blink. Style `cute` (`blob_face_head`) keeps the round mascot eyes for balls, slimes and small pets: on a sphere head pass `shape: "cap", taper: 0` to `world3d.scene.face`.

## Review round (reviewer measured from stills, applied)

Features sit lower on the skull (eye line 0.63 of the skull box, mouth 0.87, chin-to-mouth 13 %), the iris is taller than the opening and clipped by the lid, brows lighter, the five half-lid poses differ (asymmetric lids, a lifted corner for smug, a small o for sleepy), head visemes have a thin teeth line and a dark inside; the face patch gets no ink hull of its own (the skull's hull carries the jaw), hair strands take 0.4 of the outline width and a narrow gloss band scaled by the hair's luminance; the hairline is lower, the helmet thicker with crown volume and the fringe reaches the brows.

## Gotchas

- The primitives' `torso_top` reaches into the head; the lofted torso closes a neck's length under the chin, so collars and bows hang from `shoulder_top`.
- A `long` hair girl rigs with `neck_not_found` (the hair fills the neck notch of the silhouette): harmless, the Head joint still lands in the egg.
- Turntable copies need an `idle` beat each, or they stand in T-pose.
- The kit's picture is transparent outside the features: the 3D skin shows through, so pass `skin` equal to the head colour (the blink patch is painted in it).
- `fit` assumes the drawn skull is the egg: `ru = rv · rx / ry`; the head traits (`width 0.12`, `height 0.146–0.152`) match `blob_face_head.SKULL`.

## Round 7: a head that is an egg, arms and hair that lie on the body
- **Skull from measurements, not a formula**: `tests/test_blob_body.py::test_anime_skull_is_an_egg_measured_from_anime_heads_not_a_ball` pins the proportions (width/height 0.78–0.88, widest ring at 50–70 % of the height, jaw ring under 0.62 of the widest at 10 % up, crown ring about half of it at 95 %). To re-measure or change the target, draw bald front/side sheets with `generation.image` and compare silhouettes (the front outline of the table matches the Qwen sheets to within a few pixels at 520 px per head height).
- **Face UVs are isotropic**: `face_patch` maps x and y with the same scale (`ry`, `rv`), so a narrower skull does not squash the drawn eyes.
- **Arms**: a shoulder cap (1.2x) tapering to the elbow, a slim wrist (0.58x), a fuller mitten with short fat fingers (an edge-on hand is no longer a spike). Thighs under a skirt or shorts take the garment colour and the pelvis is at least as wide as the legs' tops, so no box shows beside the waist.
- **Hair**: `curve_tube(hug=head centre)` lays each clump as a ribbon along the skull (thin side away from it); before, bangs were blades seen edge-on and read as bars. `short` keeps crown clumps, the other styles a smooth dome. (Superseded below by strand cards.)
- **Shirt V**: the shirt under an open jacket is its own panel a hair outside the torso (`_shirt_v`), not a colour change by vertex (it blurred into a glow or jagged when the torso had more segments).
- **Feminine and masculine heads**: `blob_loft.SKULLS` has three measured tables selected by the head `kind` (`fem`, `boy`, `man`; `model3d.anime.make` sets it from `gender`): `girl` is wide and soft (width 0.82 of the height), `boy` a longer head (0.71) with a clean V to a point, `man` (the Lucky-style hero) a long narrow head (0.69) with a brow ridge, straight jaw lines to a flat chin plate and a thick neck (`NECK_SHARE`), face shape `sharp` by default. Depth is about 0.9 of the height for all (`sz` may now be up to 1.3 of the width). To add a kind, draw bald front and side sheets with `generation.image`, measure them like `blobd/measure_ref.py` did, add a table with the same rows and a `NECK_SHARE` entry.
- **Hair as strand cards (round 8)**: every lock is a curved card (`blob_hair._card`: three segments across, bowed outward, roots a shade darker, both faces, `u` across and `v` from root to tip) in the `hair` primitive, with texture coordinates (`UVMesh`; the scalp gets strands running from the crown down the head, mirrored so there is no seam). `ui/src/features/scene3d/hairStrands.ts` gives that material a generated map when a model loads (`applyHairStrands` in `loadSlotGltf`, so the editor and the export agree): grey multipliers of the hair colour (streaks, a light seam between strands) and an alpha that narrows each lock to a frayed tip, cut out with `alphaTest 0.5`, two-sided; a cut-out material gets no ink hull, so hair has no outline. Hair written before the cards (no `uv`) keeps its plain colour. Density: eleven fringe locks, fourteen back locks for `long`, a second shorter side lock. Limits: no motion, no per-strand colour, the cards are 7 rows long.

## Realistic style (PS2-era): a mould and its textures

`style: "real"` makes a person in the spirit of a PS2/early PS3 game (a low-poly body with a photographic face and printed clothes), in **two parts you can change independently**:

1. **The mould** (`model3d.anime.make`): a lofted, rigged body with real proportions (broad shoulders for a man, `man` and `boy` head shapes, a real nose, a plain body: no collar or tie, hair optional) and UVs into a texture **atlas**. It is the same mould whatever it wears. Traits: `gender` (`man|woman`), `body`, `height`, `bottom` (`trousers|skirt|shorts`, the only clothing that is geometry), `skin`, `hair_style` (`none` when the head picture has its hair painted), `face_source`.
2. **The textures** (any picture of the atlas): the reply gives `guide_body_file` (2048x1024: front view left, back view right, in the body's T-pose) and `guide_head_file` (2048x1024: three portrait tiles, the head and neck from the front, the right side and the back). They are shaded grey pictures of the mould in exactly the atlas layout. **Repaint each with `generation.image`** (`image_refs: [guide]`, `video_prompt_type: "KI"`, `resolution: "2048x1024"`, Qwen) with a prompt such as *"Flat colour texture map for a realistic PS2-era 3D game character, the full body front view on the left and the back view on the right, exactly the same T-pose, silhouette and layout as the reference image, painted over the grey mannequin. A man in … Even flat lighting, no shading, plain dark background"* and, for the head, *"Three photographic portrait textures of the head and neck of the same realistic man …, side by side in three tall panels with exactly the same silhouettes and layout as the reference image … left: from the front, middle: pure right profile facing left, right: the back"*. Then call `model3d.anime.make` again with the same traits plus `texture` (the body picture) and `texture_head` (the head picture, optional: without it the head tiles are dark). The picture is written into the GLB (`attach_texture`), vertex colours stop tinting it, the pictures bleed over the ground around the figure so the mould's edges never sample the background. **To change the clothes, repaint only the body guide and call make again**; to change the face, repaint the head guide.

**Draped, not stuck on.** A viewer projects the atlas per pixel (`ui/src/features/scene3d/atlasProjection.ts`, applied when a model loads, so the editor and the export agree): it reads the bind-pose position and normal, samples the front, side and back views and blends them by how the surface faces each one (the glTF material carries the frame in `extras.atlas`), so the picture turns round the head and the body like cloth, with no seam and no oval face mask. The UVs below are the fallback for a viewer without the shader (and what the guides were drawn with).

**A face, not a mask: relief from the painting's own depth.** When `texture_head` is given, `make` runs the front tile of the head picture through Depth Anything V2 (base, **CPU**, about 4 s: `blob_face_relief`, checkpoint `ckpts/depth/depth_anything_v2_vitb.pth`; without it the face stays flat) and keeps only what stands out of the low-pass of the depth over the head (the nose, the brows, the cheekbones, the lips, the chin: the head's roundness is the skull's). The face of the mould becomes a dense patch (46 degrees each way, `face_relief_patch`) pushed forward along +Z only by that relief (a nose tip is `NOSE_SHARE` = 0.10 of the head's height), so the picture stays exactly on its place and the geometry agrees with the paint from the front to about 50 degrees; at the border the relief fades to zero, melting into the skull. The modelled nose of the plain mould is dropped (the painting brings its own). The viewer keeps the patch on the front tile whole and blends the skull round it by the angle from the face (front to side between 46 and 58 degrees, to the back between 112 and 140), not by the normal, so there is no oval and no second copy of the nose. Cut the face out of the head and paint it straight onto the cut piece, or fade a mask into the skin: both were considered; the relief patch is the cut piece, and its border carries no visible seam because the skull under it shows the same front picture.

How the atlas works (`blob_texture`): the top half is an orthographic projection of the T-pose seen from the front and from behind (a triangle takes the view it faces, whole); the bottom half holds the head at three times that scale (front tile for triangles within 45 degrees of the face, side tile for the sides, mirrored on the left, back tile for the back; a face patch of 52 degrees each way sits on the front tile; the jaw's underside and the crown take the front tile). Skinned: the UVs are fixed per vertex, so the picture follows every clip. Render without ink for a PS2 look: `toon {steps 4, outline 0, shade "classic"}` and `look.exposure` about 1.5.

Limits: where two views blend (about 35 degrees from the front) the front-to-side band (46 to 58 degrees) shows a thin seam where the painted front and side portraits disagree; the relief is a single-view depth estimate, so a nose seen in profile is the front picture stretched over the bump (a smear of skin tones, not a drawn profile); hands and shoes are blocky; the face is a picture, so expressions and lipsync mouths need a face kit made from the photo (not done yet: a `photo` face source with warped mouths); a woman's mould is not tuned as much as the man's.

### A realistic character that talks (photo face kit)
`model3d.anime.make` with `texture_head` also writes `face_pose_file` (the front third of the head picture centred on a square 1024 canvas, the same pixels the relief patch shows). Make a kit from it with the app's own tools: `characters.save` (a cutout character whose `base` is that file) and `characters.rig.flat` with `style: {"mouthStyle": "warp"}` (DWPose finds the mouth of a photograph; `hints` fix it when not). The warp mouths are the photograph's own pixels pushed apart (the closed mouth is the picture unchanged, an open one is a dark gap with muted teeth). Then in a Video 3D scene `world3d.scene.face {object_id, kit_id, shape: "mesh", center, radii}` **without `skin`** (a kit with no skin colour is cut out at its edges so the skull's own picture shows through), and `world3d.scene.talk` with `blink: false` (the rig's blink is a flat skin patch that reads as pasted on a photograph). The viewer samples the kit's canvas through the patch's UVs while the face kit is on (`atlasProjection.useMapUv`), and goes back to the atlas when it is removed. Scripted example: the session scratchpad `blobd/realtalk.py`; sample: `real_ps2_habla.mp4` (three shots, 10 s).
Limits: expressions are not drawn (only the mouth moves); the rig's blink is unusable on a photograph; at 30 degrees the temple shows the seam of the front and side pictures; the head turns with the 'talk' clip, which looks stiff at medium distance.

### Underside of the head and the hair (round after the first talking video)
- **What faces down takes the neck's skin.** A front projection smears the beard's row over the underside of the jaw and the chin (a dark slab seen from below). `make` stores the head picture's neck colour in the atlas extras (`neck_skin`: the median of the row at 0.76 to 0.84 of the front tile under the beard) and the viewer reads the neck row of the front tile at the same x for every surface whose normal points down (`underside`, from -0.45 to -0.8), so the chin and jaw underside are the neck's skin with its own shading.
- **The pale temple band.** Beyond the face patch the skull fades from the front picture to the side picture. The side picture's temple is bare forehead skin where the front picture has hair, so the blend made a pale band. Above the brows the front picture is now kept longer (`upperHead`: the side picture comes in between about 77 and 98 degrees up there, 53 to 75 below), hair stretching better than skin.
- **A hair cap for a painted face** (`hair_style: "short"` with a head picture): `blob_hair._real_cap`, a cap above the painted hairline, the flanks above the ears and the back behind them, no fringe and no locks (the painting has its own), the colour read from the head picture (`hair_of`) unless `hair_color` is given. It gives a hair silhouette instead of an egg, at the price of a flat-coloured cap beside a photographic face: the default stays `none` (painted hair).
